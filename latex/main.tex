\begin{abstract}
A computed reference library replaces a measurement with a forward model. How accurate it
must be before library search succeeds, and what its error does to abstention, has not
been established against authentic material. Eleven sealed vials of nerve, vesicant and
fourth-generation agents were measured at 1064\,nm from raw detector counts, and thirty
acquisitions queried against a measured and a calibrated 54-member density functional
theory library under one pipeline, grid and metric, none fitted to identification. The
measured library assigns all thirty correctly and the computed thirteen, or 12 to 19 over
the forward model's declared free choices; twenty-seven of twenty-seven against thirteen
once a vial found to contain benzene is set aside. The measured arm is a within-vial
repeatability ceiling: on another laboratory's band lists the two reach three of five and
two of five. Similarity to the correct entry falls from 0.983 to 0.703 while congener
similarities are comparable, so what fails is accuracy on the correct molecule, not
separation between candidates, and twelve metrics recover at most three of the errors. The
band-position residual is 0.55 of the linewidth, which is the regime in which similarity
measures are reported to misbehave. Class assignment is indistinguishable while the
molecule is in the library and falls to 19 of 27 once it is deleted, soman reading as a
vesicant in all four acquisitions. Of six abstention scores none separates the
class-absent condition without error.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
%%  introduction
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
Rapid, unambiguous identification of a chemical warfare agent (CWA) at the point of release governs every decision that follows: whether to evacuate, which prophylaxis to administer, and how to decontaminate.\cite{szinicz2005history,delfino2009organophosphorus} The analytical burden is formidable. Verification under the Chemical Weapons Convention has been described as redefining what an analytical challenge means,\cite{erickson1998cwc,uzumcu2014cwc} and the detection and destruction of these compounds has been reviewed extensively.\cite{kim2011destruction,jang2015update} The laboratory methods that carry that burden, principally chromatography--mass spectrometry, require sample transport, preparation and expert interpretation.\cite{witkiewicz2016chromatographic,singh2016analysis,reddy2004trace,terzic2015identification}

Raman spectroscopy sits differently in this picture. It is non-contact, needs no sample preparation, penetrates transparent and translucent containers, and resolves closely related molecules through their vibrational fingerprints. Portable Raman analyzers are now standard in military and first-responder inventories, and have been applied to CWA samples directly, including the attribution of sulfur mustard to its synthesis route from portable-instrument spectra.\cite{wiktelius2018portable} Our own group has developed complementary field-deployable approaches based on colorimetric and optical sensor arrays coupled to machine learning,\cite{hazref,kim2025multiplex,group3ref} on real-time image-based recognition,\cite{ondeviceref} and on mobile sampling platforms for volatile releases,\cite{PARK2026108691} and has worked with authentic agents in studies of photocatalytic degradation.\cite{tio2,Kim10072026}

Yet field instruments fail for a reason that has little to do with sensitivity: \textbf{they cannot identify an agent that is not already present in their reference library.}\cite{chemosensors14090189} The problem is recognized across sensing modalities, and has motivated open-set and out-of-distribution formulations in which an unfamiliar analyte is flagged as such rather than forced onto the nearest known label.\cite{KANG2026143199,drift} Reference libraries are assembled from measured spectra, and measuring an authentic CWA requires licensed facilities, controlled-substance authorization, and safety infrastructure that few laboratories possess. The consequence is a structural gap. The agents of greatest concern are precisely those least likely to appear in any commercial library: novel analogs, degradation products, and the fourth-generation Novichok (A-series) agents.\cite{kloske2019novichoks,nepovimova2018novichok,franca2019novichoks,noga2023novichoks,chai2018novichok} The Novichok agents were designed for exactly this circumstance: to fall outside scheduled lists and to evade standard detection equipment,\cite{mirzayanov2008state,bolt2022recent} and their use in the Salisbury and Amesbury incidents demonstrated the operational cost of that gap.\cite{steindl2021novichok,haslam2022cbrn}

Quantum-chemical calculation offers a route around this constraint, because a spectrum can be computed for a molecule that has never been handled. The approach is established outside the CWA domain: Taghizadeh et al. computed resonant Raman spectra for 733 monolayer materials and demonstrated automated identification of an unknown from an input experimental spectrum,\cite{taghizadeh2020library} and comparable high-throughput databases now exist for inorganic solids.\cite{liang2019highthroughput} Within the CWA field, vibrational spectra have been computed for individual agents and their simulants,\cite{mott2012calculated,ewig1985abinitio,ault2004matrix} and high-resolution gas-phase measurements exist for a few of them,\cite{walker2001rotational,suenram2002fourier} but this work has served band assignment rather than library construction; broader computational effort has concentrated on structure, toxicity and hydrolysis.\cite{bhakhoa2019theoretical,jeong2019theoretical,imrit2020theoretical,otsuka2021theoretical} The exception is the DFT--spectroscopy integrated identification method (D-SIIM) of Jeong et al., which combines experimental GC-MS, NMR and IR with computed NMR/IR predictions to assign unknown mixtures.\cite{jeong2024dsiim}

Three obstacles have kept this idea from being tested where it matters most.

\textbf{First, validation has relied on simulants.} D-SIIM was demonstrated on \textit{ethyl propyl sulfide} and methylphosphonic derivatives rather than on the agents themselves,\cite{jeong2024dsiim} and the same is true of most machine-learning CWA detection studies. The practice is understandable and widespread,\cite{witkiewicz2018analysis} but it carries a specific liability for vibrational spectroscopy. Simulants are chosen for reduced toxicity, which necessarily means an altered molecular skeleton: a simple sulfide in place of bis(2-chloroethyl) sulfide, a phosphonate ester in place of a phosphonofluoridate. Because a Raman spectrum reports directly on that skeleton, agreement obtained with a simulant does not transfer to the agent; computed infrared spectra of agents and their simulants differ substantially for precisely this reason.\cite{mott2012calculated} Authentic agents have themselves been measured: Kondo et al. recorded Raman spectra of twenty-two authentic agents with a portable instrument at 785 and 1064 nm, establishing what the technique delivers on real material.\cite{kondo2018analysis} That set contains no fourth-generation agent, and the published vibrational data for the A-series are computational throughout,\cite{vieira2021electronic} a gap a recent review attributes to the scarcity of reference material under the Chemical Weapons Convention.\cite{cekovic2025detection} Whether a computed library can identify an authentic agent has, to our knowledge, never been established, and neither has the size of the penalty incurred by replacing measured references with computed ones.

\textbf{Second, computed and measured Raman spectra are not directly comparable.} Harmonic frequencies from density functional theory systematically overestimate observed wavenumbers, and empirical scale factors are required to correct them.\cite{scott1996harmonic,rauhut1995transferable,halls2001harmonic,andersson2005newscale,merrick2007evaluation,alecu2010computational} A gas-phase single-conformer calculation carries no condensed-phase linewidth. And the quantity that electronic-structure codes report is the Raman activity $(\partial\alpha/\partial Q)^2$, not the scattered intensity a spectrometer records.\cite{komornicki1979efficient} Each discrepancy is individually well understood, but no procedure has been established for combining them into a library that can be queried with a measured spectrum. The obvious way to combine them, tuning the corrections until identification works, is also the way to make the resulting accuracy uninterpretable, since the reported performance then becomes the objective the parameters were chosen to maximize.

\textbf{Third, how the comparison is scored is itself unsettled.} Library search reduces two spectra to a scalar, and the choice of scalar is not neutral. Benchmarking eight similarity measures against noise, baseline and wavenumber displacement, Mostafapour et al.\ find that none always works better than the others and that the response to displacement is not monotone: beyond small shifts every measure reverses and reports spectra as \emph{more} alike the further apart their bands are placed.\cite{mostafapour2026raman} Rahimi et al.\ reach a compatible conclusion on measured spectra with four measures and the same one-to-one assignment used here, finding that the measures using both position and intensity rank candidate structures best.\cite{rahimi2026spectral} Their principal test bed is synthetic, built from semi-empirical (GFN2-xTB) line lists broadened with a Voigt profile: a computed forward model of the same construction as the library examined here, perturbed by design rather than by physics. A computed library adds that the frequency residual, the intensity model and the linewidth are each wrong by an unknown amount because the calculation makes them so, and it supplies the control a synthetic design cannot have, a measured library of the same molecules carried through the identical code. Whether the failure of a computed library belongs to the forward model or to the metric used to query it is therefore to be answered by sweeping the metric on real spectra and not by choosing one.

Here we measure both sides of that question. We measured Raman spectra of eleven vials of authentic agent spanning three classes: the nerve agents GD, GF and VX, the vesicants HD, HN1, HN2 and HN3, and the Novichok agents A-230, A-232, A-234 and A-242. Ten of the eleven are consistent with the agent on their label, in the sense that nothing in their spectra contradicts it; the vial labeled HN2 is not, as Section~\ref{sec:materials} shows, and the material measured was benzene. Excitation was at 1064 nm, which suppresses the fluorescence that otherwise dominates spectra of colored or partially degraded material and permits non-destructive measurement through containers.\cite{wiktelius2018portable} We then constructed a 54-member library at B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p)\cite{becke1993density,lee1988development,stephens1994abinitio} using ORCA,\cite{neese2020orca,neese2022software} comprising these agents together with organophosphorus analogs that serve as distractors, and asked what a measured and a computed library each deliver on the same queries.

The calibration that bridges the two contains \textbf{no parameter fitted to identification}. Frequency scale factors minimize the difference between measured and computed band positions, an observable independent of the performance we report; the condensed-phase linewidth is taken from the measured band widths; and the activity-to-intensity conversion introduces no freedom. Every accuracy below is therefore an outcome of the calibration and not a target of it. Under it the computed and measured band positions agree to 12.2\,\wn{} over the 94 band pairs matching within the pairing tolerance, about 2.1 pixels on the native grid, and the two libraries are queried with the same thirty acquisitions through the same code (Section~\ref{sec:headtohead} onward, Table~\ref{tab:headline}). We report the protocol, the performance and its limits, and a mechanistic account of the failures, in order to establish how accurate a forward model must be before a computed reference library can be queried, and how far short of that a standard one falls.

\subsection{Safety statement}

All chemical warfare agents were handled exclusively by trained personnel at the CBRN Defense Research Institute, a laboratory designated by the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) and currently designated following the 2026 proficiency test, under the access control, engineering controls, personal protective equipment\cite{ticcwaref} and decontamination procedures established there for authentic agents. No agent left the facility; only spectral data files were exported for analysis. Agent syntheses, purification routes, quantities and precursor information are deliberately omitted.

\subsection{Raman measurement of authentic agents}

Raman spectra were acquired from eleven vials of authentic agent covering three toxicological classes: the nerve agents GD (soman), GF (cyclosarin) and VX; the vesicants HD (sulfur mustard), HN1, HN2 and HN3 (nitrogen mustards); and the fourth-generation Novichok agents A-230, A-232, A-234 and A-242. Ten of the eleven are referred to below by the agent they contain. The eleventh, labeled HN2, is shown in this section to have been measured as benzene rather than as neat HN2, and is referred to throughout as the vial labeled HN2.\cite{kloske2019novichoks,noga2023novichoks}

Measurements were made on a Metrohm TacticID-1064 ST handheld Raman analyzer with 1064 nm excitation ($\pm$0.2 nm), fitted with the vial adapter supplied for the instrument. Near-infrared excitation was chosen deliberately: at 1064 nm the fluorescence that overwhelms visible-excitation Raman in colored or partially degraded samples is largely suppressed, and the instrument acquires spectra non-destructively through transparent and opaque containers, which matters where the sample cannot be purified, re-prepared or opened freely.

Each agent was measured as the neat material at ambient temperature. At least 1.0 mL was placed in a sealed 4 mL glass vial, a fill volume chosen so that variation in the depth of focus between samples is negligible, and all handling and measurement were performed inside a controlled fume enclosure. The laser was operated at 90\% of its rated maximum of 495 mW (445.5 mW) with automatic exposure control, and each sample was measured five times in succession. The exposure control sets a detector gain stage as well as an integration time; both, with the detector temperature, are recorded per acquisition in the deposited metadata (Section~S1.1 of the Supplementary Material). Spectra span 176--2500 \wn{}. The agents were synthesized at the designated laboratory rather than obtained from an outside source; A-232 was prepared immediately before its measurement and the others were drawn from laboratory stocks held under the facility's storage conditions.

No orthogonal confirmation of identity or purity was performed on any of the eleven materials for this work: there is no nuclear magnetic resonance, no gas chromatography--mass spectrometry and no titration, and no assay date or purity figure is available for the stocks. The identity of each material rests on the declared inventory and, for the six agents that Kondo et al.\ also measured, on agreement with their published band positions (Section~S10); for the four A-series agents there is no independent measured reference anywhere in the literature, and identity rests on the inventory alone. This is a limitation of the study and not of the method, but it bounds what the measured arm can establish, and part of the measured-to-computed disagreement may belong to the samples rather than to the calculation. One consequence has to be drawn rather than left for the reader: the inventory is the warrant for the A-series identities, and this work shows that warrant failing for one vial of eleven, since the material labeled HN2 is benzene. We know of no reason to doubt the other ten, but a reader is entitled to weigh the measured arm's 30 of 30 against the fact that an inventory record in this set was wrong once.

For each agent, three to five of the repeat acquisitions passed instrument quality control and were retained. Each is a separate interrogation of the same sealed vial with its own timestamp and session identifier, not an independent preparation. HD and VX yield a single genuine acquisition and have no same-vial cosine. Of the nine that do, six are stationary across repeats, with same-vial cosines of 0.998 or better; A-230 sits at 0.958 with a flat count rate, and for A-234 and A-242 the count rate rises by a factor of 12 and 23 between the first acquisition and the later ones and the cosine falls to 0.805 and 0.911. These are also the only two vials whose acquisitions span a change in detector configuration (A-242 at gain 46700 and offset 4000 for its long exposure and 45250 and 2955 for the two short ones; A-234 at offset 2955 against 2619 for the other four), but neither change can produce the spread: the gain difference is multiplicative and 3.2\,\%, to which normalization and the cosine are invariant, and the offset is a constant pedestal removed by the baseline step. The early acquisitions are therefore a different state of the sample and not a noisier measurement of the same one, and Section~S9 reports the spread for every vial. The agent, not the acquisition, is the grouping variable for every cross-validation below. All acquisitions belong to one measurement campaign, so day-to-day variability was not sampled; this is examined in the Results.

\subsection{From raw counts to analyzed spectra}

This work begins at the instrument's raw detector counts. Fifty-five files were delivered; thirty-two carry the integer detector counts an acquisition produces and are used here. The remaining twenty-three reproduce a genuine file to a multiplicative factor near unity with an added offset and noise, and are excluded throughout so that no perturbed copy of a spectrum appears in the analysis. Nine agents retain three to five genuine acquisitions; HD and VX retain one each and are never used as queries.

The detector carries 512 pixels, of which 270 fall in the 250--1800 \wn{} fingerprint region at a spacing that varies from 4.6 to 7.1 \wn{} and averages 5.73 \wn{}. All comparisons are made on that native axis: the computed spectra are brought down onto the measured grid and the measurement is never interpolated onto a finer one. The per-file integration time ranges from 119 to 30\,000 ms and is reported alongside every per-agent quantity.

Each acquisition was processed by dark-count subtraction with the instrument's own dark column; cosmic-ray removal by replicate consensus, interpolating only pixels flagged in one acquisition and not in its replicates; masking and interpolation of three detector pixels identified as defective by a criterion fixed in advance; truncation to 250--1800 \wn{}; background removal by asymmetric least squares with $\lambda = 10^{4}$ and $p = 0.01$, taken from the literature and fixed before any identification was run; and normalization to the maximum, with standard-normal-variate normalization reported alongside. The specification and the sensitivity of every step are in the Supplementary Material.

The linewidth $\Gamma$ = 22 \wn{} is measured from the spectra and not fitted to identification, but it is an apparent width that includes the instrument response. One acquisition bounds that contribution: the vial labeled HN2 contains liquid benzene, whose four bands stand at 15 to 468 standard deviations of the local noise, and the shared-width fit returns 9.1 to 9.3 \wn{} at $R^2$ = 0.983 for all three acquisitions, the narrowest in the set. That is an upper bound limited by sampling and not a determination --- at 9\,\wn{} a band is barely two points across its half-width on a grid of 5.73\,\wn{} --- and a single Lorentzian fitted to what is physically a Voigt profile does not return the Gaussian instrument width, so the decomposition is indicative. Benzene's intrinsic widths here are a few \wn{}, so the instrument contribution is of order 9 \wn{} and the adopted 22 \wn{} is not instrument-dominated. A certified shift standard would settle it.

\subsection{Data quality criterion}

A quality criterion was defined before the identification analysis: a measured spectrum was retained only if at least four bands reached 10\% of its strongest band in the 250--1800 \wn{} region. It is the only criterion used. Ten of the eleven agents satisfied it, exhibiting 7 to 20 bands above that threshold. The vial labeled HN2 yielded a single band at that threshold and is the one failure; it also lacks the low-frequency structure present in every other nitrogen mustard. An instrumental explanation is not supported, since the vial was acquired between successful HN3 and HN1 acquisitions three and two minutes away. Its four features lie at 605.6, 993.6, 1176.3 and 1585.4\,\wn{}, the four Raman-active fundamentals of liquid benzene (606, 992, 1178 and 1586\,\wn{}) to within 1.7\,\wn{} and in benzene's intensity order, and the three positions a monosubstituted ring would require, 702, 787 and 1030\,\wn{}, sit at no more than 1.6 standard deviations of the local noise, which separates benzene from toluene, chlorobenzene, pyridine and polystyrene (Section~S4.2). The vial therefore does not contain neat HN2. It is not empty of the labeled agent either: of the four bands Kondo et al.\ report for HN2 at this excitation, two are present at three standard deviations or better in every acquisition (733.0 against 729\,\wn{} and 1447.6 against 1448\,\wn{}) and a third in two of three.\cite{kondo2018analysis} They fall below the absolute prominence floor used elsewhere only because that floor is referenced to the benzene band, which carries 80\% of the intensity; the automatic exposure control set 119\,ms for this vial against 1014\,ms for HN1 and 5840\,ms for HN3, so a minor component is pushed below detection by construction. These data establish that the material measured was benzene and that HN2 dissolved in it can be neither confirmed nor excluded; an orthogonal assay or a re-measurement at a fixed longer exposure would settle it. Failing the criterion does not remove the vial. It is retained in both libraries and among the thirty queries so that the libraries face an identical query set, and every headline figure is reported with and without it. It is set aside only where its unknown identity would leave a quantity undefined: the per-agent window analyses, the in-library and class-absent conditions of the abstention analysis (where it is a condition of its own), and the correlation against molecular size. It contributes one of the 94 band pairs to the calibration, and holding it out leaves the factors and the RMSE unchanged at 1.014, 0.980 and 12.17\,\wn{}.

\subsection{Quantum-chemical calculations}

Molecular geometries were constructed from the accepted structures of each agent. Structures for the Novichok A-series follow the IUPAC names given for A-230, A-232, A-234 and A-242\cite{mirzayanov2008state,kloske2019novichoks,bhakhoa2019theoretical}: the amidine terminal amine is \textit{N,N}-diethyl in A-230, A-232 and A-234, and A-242 is the corresponding bis(diethylamino) guanidine. Initial coordinates were generated with RDKit\cite{rdkit} and pre-optimized with MMFF94.

All electronic-structure calculations used ORCA 6.1.1\cite{neese2020orca,neese2022software} at B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p).\cite{becke1993density,lee1988development,stephens1994abinitio} Each molecule was optimized with tight convergence (\texttt{TightOpt}) followed by a numerical frequency calculation (\texttt{NumFreq}) with polarizability derivatives (\texttt{\%elprop Polar 1}), yielding harmonic wavenumbers with Raman activities; structures with imaginary frequencies were rejected. Calculations used eight MPI processes with 3000 MB per core.

The library comprises 54 molecules. Eleven are the measured agents, including HN2, which failed the data-quality criterion and whose computed entry is the free base, and VX, which has a single genuine acquisition and is retained without being queried. The remainder are organophosphorus analogs of the form R$^{1}$O--P(R$^{2}$)(=O)F, generated as an 8 $\times$ 8 grid of alkyl substituents (methyl, ethyl, \textit{n}-propyl, \textit{iso}-propyl, \textit{iso}-butyl, \textit{sec}-butyl, cyclopentyl, cyclohexyl) restricted to $\leq$28 atoms. GF is simultaneously a measured agent and the cyclohexyl/methyl member of that grid, and is counted once. The analogs are plausible congeners of the G-series agents included as distractors: except for GF none is ever the correct answer, and their role is to test whether identification degrades as the library grows. The library is strongly skewed by class --- 46 of the 54 members are nerve agents, 4 are vesicants and 4 are Novichok agents --- so with 85\% of the library carrying the nerve-agent label a nerve agent has correspondingly more opportunities to be matched within its own class, and class assignments must be read against that composition.

\subsection{Spectral representation and calibration}

Computed line spectra were converted to continuous spectra on the instrument's own 270-point axis and compared over 250--1800 \wn{}, the range common to calculation and measurement. This window contains the diagnostic bands used throughout --- P--F and the coupled skeletal modes, P--O--C and P=O for the organophosphorus agents, C--S and C--Cl for the vesicants, C=N for the Novichok series --- and excludes the C--H stretching region, which lies above the 2500 \wn{} limit of the measurement in any case and is dominated by alkyl content rather than molecular identity.

Three physically motivated corrections were applied, none with a parameter selected using an identification result.

\textbf{(i) Frequency scaling.} Harmonic wavenumbers were scaled for the systematic overestimation of the harmonic approximation; published factors for this level of theory lie near 0.97 for fundamentals but are dominated by X--H stretches, and mode-dependent factors have long been recommended.\cite{scott1996harmonic,rauhut1995transferable,halls2001harmonic,andersson2005newscale,merrick2007evaluation,alecu2010computational} Two factors were used, $s_{\mathrm{low}}$ below 800 \wn{} and $s_{\mathrm{high}}$ above it, obtained by minimizing the root-mean-square difference between measured band maxima and scaled harmonic wavenumbers over all agents with a computed entry, paired one-to-one within 35 \wn{}. Band position is an observable of the measurement logically independent of the identification performance reported in the Results, so the fit introduces no circularity. The optimum is $s_{\mathrm{low}}$ = 1.014 and $s_{\mathrm{high}}$ = 0.980. A range-dependent map is the weaker of the forms a recent measured-Raman benchmark compares, which reports the mode-dependent form as the most accurate;\cite{rahimi2026spectral} we fitted that form too, which needs no new quantum chemistry, and it does not improve on the adopted map (Section~\ref{sec:modescale}, Section~S3.6).

\textbf{(ii) Condensed-phase linewidth.} Each mode was broadened with a Lorentzian of full width at half maximum $\Gamma$, representing the broadening absent from a gas-phase calculation. $\Gamma$ was not fitted: it was set to 22 \wn{}, the rounded median (21.7 \wn{}) of shared-width Lorentzian fits to the detected bands of each genuine acquisition, whose interquartile range is 18.2--26.5 \wn{} and whose per-acquisition values span 9.1--48.4 \wn{}.

\textbf{(iii) Raman scattering factor.} Electronic-structure codes report the Raman activity, proportional to the square of the polarizability derivative, which is not what a spectrometer records.\cite{komornicki1979efficient} The activity $S_i$ was converted to a scattered intensity using

\[
I_i \;\propto\; \frac{(\nu_0-\nu_i)^4}{\nu_i}\,\frac{S_i}{1-\exp(-hc\nu_i/k_\mathrm{B}T)},
\]

with $\nu_0 = 9398$ \wn{} (1064 nm excitation) and $T$ = 298 K, combining the fourth-power scattering dependence with the Bose--Einstein population factor.

The calibration has no parameter fitted to identification, but it is not free of the test set: pairing a measured maximum with a scaled harmonic wavenumber requires knowing which computed molecule the measurement belongs to, so the labels of all eleven computed agents enter the fit. Refitting with each agent excluded in turn moves $s_\mathrm{low}$ over 1.006--1.024 and $s_\mathrm{high}$ over 0.980--0.984 (ranges of 1.8\% and 0.4\%), and rerunning the whole identification under each held-out calibration returns 13 of 30 for nine of the eleven and 12 for the other two, with the class figure between 25 and 26. Those refits land on only four distinct values of $s_\mathrm{low}$, so we also scanned the envelope on a 0.002 grid: the molecular figure runs from 12 to 16 of 30, reaching 16 at $s_\mathrm{low}$ = 1.016 and 1.018 where all three HN1 acquisitions become correct, and $s_\mathrm{high}$ moves nothing. The adopted 13 of 30 therefore sits in the lower part of a 12 to 16 range and is not the most favorable figure available inside the calibration's own uncertainty. The refit is a stability check and not a leakage control: $\Gamma$ and the band positions entering the fit come from the same acquisitions later queried and are not held out. The resulting parameters are applied unchanged to all 54 library members, and every accuracy reported is an outcome of this procedure and not a target of it.

The alternative is available and produces a better-looking number. Selecting ($s$, $\Gamma$) by a grid search that maximizes top-1 accuracy over the thirty queries reaches 16 of 30 at $s$ = 0.968, $\Gamma$ = 46 \wn{}, against 13 for the position-fitted calibration. We decline the three queries: the optimum is the training score of a two-parameter fit, reached in only 3 of the 441 grid cells (Table S13), and moving $s$ by 0.4\% in either direction returns 13 of 30. Measured spectra, preprocessed as above, were normalized to unit maximum on the same native axis and never interpolated; no further baseline correction or smoothing was applied at this stage.

\subsection{Identification and statistical validation}

Identification was performed by cosine similarity between a measured spectrum and each calibrated library spectrum, the highest-scoring member being the top-1 assignment, at two levels: molecular (is the assigned molecule correct?) and class (is it in the correct toxicological class?).

Three further tests were applied. First, to probe the case the method exists to serve, in which a query's molecule is in no library, each agent's own computed entry was deleted before that agent was queried and the class of the best remaining match recorded. Second, the same deletion was applied to an entire toxicological class. Third, abstention was evaluated with six confidence scores: the top-1 cosine; the margin, the top-1 similarity minus the runner-up's; a peak-to-sidelobe ratio, the top-1 similarity minus the mean of the rest divided by their standard deviation; the top-1 similarity standardized against the within-library similarities of the matched entry; the residual of a non-negative least-squares unmixing of the query onto the library; and the fraction of detected measured bands with a paired computed band. Each was assessed by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve separating class-present from class-absent, by whether the two are completely separable, and at the operating point set by the median of the class-present distribution. Every identification is performed on individual acquisitions; agent-mean spectra are used only for the frequency calibration and the region-resolved comparison. A further analysis asks whether the discrimination reflects molecular identity and not measurement provenance: session-mean spectra were compared pairwise and the cosines partitioned into same agent and different session, same class and different agent, and different class, then compared with a one-sided Mann--Whitney \textit{U} test.

Band positions for the computed-versus-measured comparison were located with a local-maximum criterion (topographic prominence > 0.05 of the normalized maximum, minimum separation 2 grid points), applied identically to the measured spectra and to the computed spectra on the same grid. Three stricter criteria and a narrower tolerance are reported in the Supplementary Material; the fitted scale factors move by at most 1.0\% across the sixteen combinations and by at most 0.8\% across the eight using the adopted criterion. Maxima were paired one-to-one as a linear assignment problem on the matrix of absolute wavenumber differences with pairings beyond 35 \wn{} forbidden, which is stricter than nearest-neighbor matching, under which one computed band may be claimed by several measured bands with residuals of opposite sign that cancel. Analysis code and the calibrated spectral library are provided in the Supplementary Material.

\subsection{Authentic agents, raw acquisitions and the computational library}

Eleven sealed vials of authentic agent spanning three toxicological classes were
measured as neat materials at 1064\,nm: the nerve agents GD (soman), GF (cyclosarin)
and VX; the vesicants HD (sulfur mustard) and the nitrogen mustards HN1, HN2 and HN3;
and the fourth-generation Novichok agents A-230, A-232, A-234 and
A-242.\cite{kloske2019novichoks,noga2023novichoks} Ten are consistent with their own labels; the vial labeled HN2 was measured as benzene, as Section~\ref{sec:materials} sets out. The analysis in this work begins at
the instrument's raw detector counts and not at a reduced spectrum, and the first
result is an accounting of what those files contain.

\textbf{Thirty-two of the fifty-five files are genuine acquisitions.} The remaining
twenty-three are derived copies of a genuine file. Each reproduces its parent to a
multiplicative factor near unity with an added offset and noise, and none carries the
integer detector counts that an acquisition produces. They are excluded throughout.
Nine agents retain three to five genuine acquisitions; HD and VX retain one each, and
are therefore present in the libraries but never used as queries. Every count reported
below refers to genuine acquisitions only.

\textbf{The measurement grid is coarser than the reduced files suggest.} The detector
carries 512 pixels, of which 270 fall in the 250--1800\,\wn{} fingerprint region, at a
spacing that varies from 4.6 to 7.1\,\wn{} and averages 5.73\,\wn{}. In the
600--850\,\wn{} window the spacing is 6.3\,\wn{}. All comparisons in this work are made
on that native axis: the computed spectra are brought down onto the measured grid, and
the measurement is never interpolated onto a finer one. A band-position residual of
12.2\,\wn{} is therefore approximately 2.1 pixels, and we read it as such.

\textbf{The wavenumber axis agrees with an in-campaign reference liquid and with an
independent measurement of the same compounds.} No certified Raman shift standard was run
on this instrument. Benzene is not among the eight materials ASTM E1840 recommends for
that purpose, and that guide addresses neither traceability nor
uncertainty.\cite{ntziouni2022standards} An in-campaign check is nevertheless available,
because the vial labeled HN2 contains liquid benzene and was acquired between the HN3 and
HN1 acquisitions, three and two minutes away. Its four strong bands fall at 605.6, 993.6,
1176.3 and 1585.4\,\wn{} against the benzene fundamentals at 606, 992, 1178 and
1586\,\wn{},\cite{zhang2011benzene} a mean residual of $-0.3$\,\wn{} and an RMS of
1.2\,\wn{} spanning 606 to 1586\,\wn{}, which contains 72 of the 94 paired bands. The
three weaker of the four stand at 15 to 39 standard deviations of the local noise, so
their positions are well determined even though only the ring-breathing band clears the
10\,\% criterion of Section~\ref{sec:materials}. Over that span an additive error on the
measured axis is bounded at about 2\,\wn{}, below half the 5.73\,\wn{} sampling. Twenty-one
of the 22 uncovered pairs lie below 606\,\wn{}, which is the region in which the
calibration is least determined, so the bound is silent exactly where it would be most
useful. We also compare against the only published Raman measurements of
authentic agents at this excitation: Kondo et al.\ report assigned band positions for twenty-two agents at
785\,nm and 1064\,nm, from material synthesized in house and confirmed by nuclear
magnetic resonance and gas chromatography--mass spectrometry above 98\%
purity.\cite{kondo2018analysis} Seven of their agents are in this set. Of the thirty-one
bands they report for those seven, twenty-two are reproduced here with a residual mean of
$-0.9$\,\wn{} and an RMS of 5.5\,\wn{}, below the 5.73\,\wn{} sampling of this
measurement and well inside the 15--18\,\wn{} resolution they quote at 1064\,nm
(Section~S10 of the Supplementary Material). The 12.2\,\wn{} measured-to-computed residual is therefore
not an artifact of this instrument's calibration. One is the exception: the vial labeled HN2 reproduces two of the four bands reported for it, and only below the prominence floor, which bears on what was in that vial rather than on the axis and is taken up in Section~S4.2 of the Supplementary Material.

\textbf{Exposure differs by more than two orders of magnitude between agents.} The
per-file integration time, recorded by the instrument, ranges from 119 to 30\,000\,ms.
It is a confounder between agents and we report it with every per-agent quantity rather
than assuming a common acquisition condition.

The design is summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:design}. The computed library comprises 54
molecules. Eleven are the measured agents. The remainder are organophosphorus
analogs of the general form R$^{1}$O--P(R$^{2}$)(=O)F, generated as an 8 $\times$ 8
grid of alkyl substituents restricted to $\leq$28 atoms, hydrogens included, and included as
distractors. The library is strongly skewed by class: 46 of the 54 members are
nerve-class, 4 are vesicants and 4 are Novichok agents, so 85\% of the library carries
the nerve-agent label and class assignments must be read against that composition.

\subsection{A preprocessing specification defined here, not inherited}

The spectra as delivered by the instrument software had been corrected for
background and rescaled by a procedure we could characterize but not specify. Working from raw
counts removes that dependency, and the specification below is stated in full so that
every step is auditable. Two of its steps produced results worth reporting in their own
right.

\textbf{Single-spectrum despiking deletes real signal.} Flagging narrow positive
excursions in one spectrum at a time marks 7.8 pixels per acquisition, 1.5\% of the
512-pixel detector. Of those flags, 81.1\% reproduce at the same pixel in an independent
acquisition of the same agent. A cosmic ray is a random event and cannot reproduce;
what reproduces is signal. Requiring replicate consensus, so that a pixel is interpolated
only when it is flagged in one acquisition and not in its replicates, reduces the removal
to 1.8 pixels per acquisition, 0.34\% of the detector. The conventional single-spectrum
treatment would have removed six pixels of real signal per acquisition from every
spectrum in this study. HD and VX have a single genuine acquisition each and cannot be
treated this way; for those two the residual cosmic-ray contamination is bounded above
by the 0.34\% rate measured on the others.

\textbf{Three detector pixels are defective, and two of them sit in diagnostic windows.}
A criterion set before the identification analysis identifies pixels at 813.7, 1220.6
and 1634.4\,\wn{}. A pixel qualifies if its deviation from a local baseline is negative
in at least 90\% of acquisitions and exceeds six local standard deviations in at least
half of them. The first lies at the upper edge of the 600--850\,\wn{} window, the
second within 1150--1400\,\wn{}, and the third at the foot of the C=N
stretching region. A sign that is fixed across every acquisition and every agent is an
instrumental property, not a chemical one. Masking and interpolating the three raises the separation between within-class and between-class similarity from 0.090 to 0.123.
The region-resolved analysis in the previous version of this work was computed with
these pixels included.



The linewidth adopted below is measured from the spectra rather than fitted, and the sensitivity of every preprocessing choice is bounded and reported in full in the Supplementary Material (S1, S3); standard-normal-variate normalization is carried alongside maximum normalization throughout rather than adopted because it scores better.
\subsection{A calibration with no identification-tuned parameter}

Three corrections take a computed harmonic line spectrum to a form comparable with a
measurement: a frequency scale, a scattering factor and a linewidth
(Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}a,b). None was chosen by looking at an identification result.

\textbf{The frequency scale is fitted to band positions only.} Band position is an
observable of the measurement and logically independent of the quantity we report.
Minimizing the root-mean-square difference between measured band maxima and scaled harmonic
wavenumbers over all agents gives $s_{\mathrm{low}}$ = 1.014 below 800\,\wn{} and
$s_{\mathrm{high}}$ = 0.980 above, with a positional RMSE of 12.2\,\wn{} over the 94 band
pairs matching within the 35\,\wn{} tolerance, about 2.1 pixels on the native grid
(Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}c). Pairing is partial --- 82 measured and 31 computed bands
are left unpaired, and the figure that charges each the full tolerance is 27.1\,\wn{}
(Section~S3.2) --- so we quote both and use the paired value only to compare calibrations
with one another. Two factors are used because the optimum is demonstrably not uniform
across the window (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}d). The resulting map is discontinuous at
the boundary, sending 800$^-$ to 811.2 and 800$^+$ to 784.0\,\wn{}, a 27.2\,\wn{} overlap
in which five of the eleven agents have a pair of modes whose order it reverses; the
window-resolved analysis was therefore repeated under the monotone affine map of
Section~S3.4, under which the phosphorus contrast in the 600--850\,\wn{} window is
$+$0.184 against $+$0.265 and remains the only window of the five where the sign runs that
way. Under the adopted map 1150--1400\,\wn{} carries a small contrast of the same sign
($+$0.027).

\textbf{The calibration is not circular.} Refitting with each agent held out in turn moves
$s_{\mathrm{low}}$ over 1.006--1.024 (1.8\,\%) and $s_{\mathrm{high}}$ over 0.980--0.984
(Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}e); no agent drives it, and the identification below is
obtained under factors effectively independent of the agent being identified.

\textbf{The scattering factor is required by physics, not fitted.} The code's activity is
converted to a cross-section by the $(\nu_0-\nu)^4/\nu$ dependence and the Bose thermal
population factor, with $\nu_0$ set by the 1064\,nm excitation and the temperature taken as
298\,K. No parameter in this step is free.

\textbf{The limit of the frequency calibration, and what it costs.} A multiplicative scale
factor is exactly degenerate with a multiplicative error in the instrument's wavenumber
axis, and no wavenumber standard was run during the campaign. The 3.5\,\% difference between
the two factors excludes a \textit{uniform} axis gain error but not an additive offset,
which is the one error that would bias this study toward its own conclusion: it cancels in a
measured-to-measured comparison and not in a measured-to-computed one. The benzene
acquisitions bound that offset at about 2\,\wn{} over 606 to 1586\,\wn{}, narrower than
every non-zero value in the scan below, so the offset arm reports a sensitivity over
offsets the measurement does not support. We did not leave it as a caveat. An affine map
$\nu_\mathrm{meas} = g\,\nu_\mathrm{harm} + \delta$ fitted to the same band set, pairing 93
under that map, with the same objective and number of free parameters, gives $g$ = 0.954 and
$\delta$ = +36\,\wn{} at an RMSE of 11.1\,\wn{}, better than the 12.2 of the adopted scaling.
By the calibration's own criterion the affine model is the better one, and the two are not
distinguishable as explanations: their predicted positions differ by 9.0\,\wn{} RMS, below
the residual either leaves, and 42 of the 94 pairs lie below 800\,\wn{}, where the design
matrix has a condition number of 2.7 $\times$ 10$^{3}$ and offset and slope correlate at
$-$0.93. The identification is not invariant to the choice, and the direction matters:
rebuilding the library under the affine calibration gives 16 of 30 rather than 13 (similarity
to the correct entry 0.702 against 0.703), and an additive error of $-$20 to $+$30\,\wn{}
on the measured axis, refitted at each value, moves the computed library between 12 and 16
of 30 while the measured library stays at 30 of 30 (Section~S3.4).

\textbf{Scaling by mode type, the form the literature prefers, is the worst of the three.}
\label{sec:modescale}
The adopted map splits on wavenumber, and the scaling papers cited in the Methods recommend
splitting on mode type; Rahimi et al.\ fit both against measured Raman and report the
mode-dependent form as the most accurate.\cite{rahimi2026spectral} We fitted it. The
diagnostic window is built from P--F, P=O, P--O and P--C stretching, so modes were split by
P--X bond-stretch character, the squared projection of each P-bonded pair's relative
displacement onto its bond axis, taken from the deposited normal modes. That character
separates cleanly where a generic stretch-versus-bend split does not --- no mode of a 25-atom
organophosphate is more than about 30\,\% pure bond stretch, while for cyclosarin the P--X
character runs 0.00--0.02 below 600\,\wn{} and 0.09--0.28 across 697--820\,\wn{}. Fitted on
the identical non-circular objective with the same two free parameters, it returns a
position RMSE of 13.7\,\wn{} against 12.2 for the adopted map and 11.1 for the affine one,
and the same 13 of 30 at the molecular level with a lower class count, 22 of 30 against 26
(Section~S3.6). Both mode classes straddle the 800\,\wn{} boundary, P--X modes spanning 447
to 1630\,\wn{} and the rest 5 to 3158, so neither can encode what the two-region map does,
that harmonic B3LYP under-scales low-wavenumber modes and over-scales high ones. On molecules
this size mode character and wavenumber are not aligned, and it is the second that the
calibration has to correct; we read this as a bound on the transferability of the
mode-dependent recommendation, which was fitted on species small enough for the two to
coincide, and not as a correction to it.

We report the adopted 13 of 30 as the headline. At the molecular level the better-fitting
affine calibration and the more favorable offsets give a higher number and the mode-resolved
map the same one, so the deficit is conservative with respect to the calibration there; at the class level both are lower, 23 for the affine map and 22 for the mode-resolved one against 26, so no such statement holds, and we do not make it. A certified shift standard would settle the question across the full
window and we recommend it for any replication.

\subsection{The same queries against a measured and a computed library}
\label{sec:headtohead}

The central comparison of this work holds everything fixed except the origin of the
library. Thirty genuine acquisitions of nine sealed vials are used as queries. Both libraries
are interrogated with the identical preprocessing, the identical native grid and the
identical cosine metric, and in neither case is the query acquisition itself present in
the library it is matched against.

\textbf{A same-vial measured library assigns all thirty acquisitions correctly; a
calibrated DFT library assigns thirteen} (Figure~\ref{fig:headtohead}a,b), and thirteen again when the full
54-member library is searched. Three of the thirty acquisitions are of the vial whose label
Section~\ref{sec:materials} shows to be unsupported. Setting them aside leaves 27 of 27
against 13 of 27, and leaves every conclusion below unchanged; because all three were
computed-library failures, the exclusion moves the computed figure from 43.3\% to 48.1\%
and the class figure from 86.7\% to 96.3\%, so it works against the claim made here rather
than for it. The reduced figures are given alongside the full ones at each headline below
and in full in Section~S10.3 of the Supplementary Material; the analysis itself is reported on the complete
thirty, so that both libraries face an identical query set. The thirty acquisitions interrogate nine sealed vials, so
the effective unit is the agent; on that unit the measured library is correct for 9 of 9
and the computed library for 4 of 9, a consistent direction on a paired exact test that
does not reach conventional significance ($b = 5$, $c = 0$, two-sided $p = 0.062$; without
HN2, $b = 4$, $c = 0$, $p = 0.125$). Interval estimates and the agent-level tabulation are
given in the Supplementary Material.

\textbf{At the level of toxicological class the two libraries are not distinguishable, so long as the molecule is in the library} (Section~\ref{sec:lomo} reports what happens when it is not). The measured library reaches 30 of 30 and the computed library 26 of 30 in the eleven-member closed set, or 27 of 27 and 26 of 27 without the benzene vial (Table~\ref{tab:headline}), and again 26 of 30 against all 54 members; on the agent unit, excluding the benzene vial, the computed library is correct for 8 of 8. Three of the four class errors are acquisitions of the benzene vial. The fourth is the second acquisition of A-234, assigned to HN1, a Novichok-to-vesicant crossing. We had attributed it to an unsettled sample, and the deposited metadata does not support that: A-234's count rate rises from 1949 to 20954\,s$^{-1}$ between its first and second acquisitions and then changes by 12\,\% over the remaining three, so the settling is complete after the first, and the second acquisition sits within 11\,\% of the later three in count rate and at the same gain and offset yet differs from them by a cosine of 0.914. The first acquisition differs from the later three by 0.805, against 0.9998 among the three later ones (Section~S9), and their measured self-similarities, 0.842 and 0.952, are the two lowest in the set. The deviation is real and we cannot explain it, so we count it as an unexplained class error of the computed library and not as an artifact of the sample. The limitation this work reports is therefore specific to identification at the molecular level; at the class level, which is what most field decisions turn on, these data do not show the computed library to be worse.

\subsection{The loss is localized, and it is largest for the phosphorus-containing agents}
\label{sec:windows}

Resolving the measured-to-computed cosine by spectral region, and taking the
measured-to-measured cosine in the same region as the ceiling that repeatability
permits, shows the deficit is not spread evenly (Figure~\ref{fig:headtohead}c).

\textbf{The deficit is not spread evenly, and the window that carries most of the
intensity is the worst.} In the 600--850\,\wn{} window the computed spectra
reach 0.723 against a repeatability ceiling of 0.982, a gap of 0.260, and that window
carries 29.2\% of the measured intensity, the largest share of any of the five. It is
also the largest gap: the remaining four windows have gaps of 0.136 (250--600\,\wn{}),
0.211 (850--1150), 0.188 (1150--1400) and 0.119 (1400--1800). The window that carries the
most intensity and the window that is reproduced worst are therefore the same one, which
is what makes the localization consequential rather than merely present. It is also, as
the next paragraph shows, the window in which the phosphorus split runs most clearly in
the direction of a deficit.

\textbf{Within that window the deficit is carried by the phosphorus-containing agents}
(Figure~\ref{fig:headtohead}f). The seven agents that contain phosphorus average
0.643 there; the three that do not average 0.908, a difference of 0.265. In three of the
four other windows the ordering reverses and the phosphorus-containing agents score
higher, by 0.042 to 0.134; in the fourth, 1150--1400\,\wn{}, the phosphorus-free agents
are higher by 0.027. Individually the phosphorus agents span 0.323 (GF) to
0.962 (VX), against 0.865--0.970 for HN3, HN1 and HD, so the spread within the
phosphorus group is more than twice the difference between the groups.

\textbf{We do not claim statistical significance for this contrast, and we state why.}
The comparison has seven molecules on one side and three on the other, so the smallest
two-sided $p$ a rank test can produce is 0.017, and five windows were examined. The
600--850\,\wn{} window returns an uncorrected two-sided $p$ of 0.117, which is not
significant before any correction, and it is not the smallest of the five: the
1400--1800\,\wn{} window returns 0.067 with the opposite sign, which is 0.337 after Holm
correction, so no window reaches the 0.05 level under any correction. This design has
essentially no power, and we report the contrast as an effect size with its sign
structure rather than as a test result. The phosphorus split is also confounded with
toxicological class, with the number of detected bands, with integration time and with
which agents have an independent published reference. The two phosphorus agents that do
have one, GD at 0.874 and VX at 0.962, sit inside the range of the phosphorus-free group.
The contrast is carried by GF, whose geometry Section~\ref{sec:geometry} shows was wrong,
and by the four A-series agents, for which no external reference and no orthogonal assay
exists. Ten molecules cannot separate these.

\textbf{Conformational flexibility does not order the deficit.} If the deficit arose from representing a flexible molecule by a single conformer, the agents with the most conformational freedom would suffer most. They do not. The number of rotatable bonds is uncorrelated with the window cosine ($r$ = +0.10 over the nine queried agents), and the 298\,K conformational entropy $S_\mathrm{conf} = -R\sum_i p_i \ln p_i$ from a GFN2 census of all eleven computed molecules gives $r$ = +0.33 with a negative leave-one-out $R^2$, so the association, such as it is, runs the wrong way. The largest population any single conformer carries, which bounds what the one structure can represent and is under a tenth for five of the ten agents, correlates at $r$ = $-$0.28. VX is the extreme: the most flexible agent, with 727 conformers within 6\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ and a perplexity $\exp(S_\mathrm{conf}/R)$ of 146, and the best of the phosphorus agents here at 0.962, while GF, with a perplexity of 3.4, is the worst at 0.323. These data do not detect an ordering by conformational freedom, which is weaker than excluding it: at ten molecules $r$ = +0.33 carries a 95\% interval of roughly $-0.37$ to $+0.80$, and the two extremes are VX, which is never queried, and the post-correction GF, whose pre-correction value is 0.583 and not 0.323. Boltzmann-weighted ensemble spectra and isomeric and conformer-selection error are tested directly in Section~\ref{sec:geometry}. The window that fails carries the P--F and P--N stretching character, but normal-mode analysis of our own structures shows the intense feature to be a mixed motion of the F--P--C fragment and not a localized P--F stretch, so the quantity implicated is the coupling among those internal coordinates and not the frequency of an isolated bond stretch.

\subsection{The result is not an artifact of the similarity metric}

A cosine between spectra broadened to 22\,\wn{} is close to a monotone function of the
band-position residual, and the residual reported above is 0.55 of that width, which is
where a cosine is most sensitive to position. Mostafapour et al.\ benchmarked eight common
measures on synthetic spectra and report that none is uniformly best and that past small
displacements every one inverts, scoring spectra as more alike as their bands move further
apart,\cite{mostafapour2026raman} so the warning applies directly. We tested it on real
spectra, with the measured library carried through the identical code as the control.

\textbf{No metric recovers the computed library, and the peak-list metrics make it worse}
(Figure~\ref{fig:metrics}a,b). Against the adopted cosine the computed library assigns 13
of 30 (13 of 27 without the benzene vial; reduced counts for every variant are in
Section~S10.3). Widening the linewidth to 40 or 50\,\wn{}, or allowing a misalignment of
$\pm$17\,\wn{}, gives 16; the peak-list hit-quality scores that commercial library search
applies give 5, 11 and 10, and the band-position Dice coefficient 15 and 11. The measured
library reaches 25 to 30 of 30 under the eight metrics for which it is defined, so the
implementations are sound, and the best of the twelve metrics recovers three of the
seventeen errors. The favourable choices do not compound: over all thirty-two combinations
of the frequency map, linewidth, metric and a responsivity ramp the molecular count is 12
to 19 of 30 and the class count 21 to 27, against 30 of 30 at both levels for the measured
library (Section~S3.5). One result in that grid runs against us: the adopted cell is not
the most favourable. Fourteen of the thirty-two cells are at least as good at both levels
and strictly better at one, and the best reaches 17 of 30 molecular and 27 of 30 class. The
adopted settings were fixed a priori --- $\Gamma$ = 22\,\wn{} is the measured median
linewidth and no ramp is the no-correction default --- and we report the range rather than
defend the point.

\textbf{Nor is it an artifact of the normalization, the spectral response, the
preprocessing or reading only the first entry.} Standard-normal-variate normalization on
both libraries leaves the computed library at 13 of 30. No relative-intensity standard was
run, so the measured intensities carry the instrument's uncorrected response while the
computed side carries a physically correct scattering model, an error that would flatter
the measured arm. Imposing a log-linear ramp on the measured spectra over end-to-end ratios
of 0.33 to 3, far wider than any plausible residual, moves the computed figure only between
11 and 14 and holds the measured arm at 30 of 30 except at the most compressive ramp, where
it is 29 (Table~S36); the class-level figure does move, from 26 of 30 to 19 at that
ramp, and we report it as responsivity-sensitive in a way the molecular figure is not.
Applying the identical baseline correction to the computed spectra, which the measurement
alone receives, lowers the agreement in every window and leaves the count at 13, so the
asymmetry works slightly in the computed library's favor. A library search also returns a
hit list: three candidates instead of one recover nine of the seventeen errors, giving a
ladder of 13, 22, 22 and 23 of 30 at one, three, five and ten, with the correct entry
outside the top ten for seven acquisitions. A hit list of three that contains the right
answer for 22 of 30 is a real capability, and it is not molecular identification of a
scheduled chemical. One of the seventeen errors is decided at a margin of 0.0031, the first
acquisition of A-234, and we report it as a tie rather than as an independent failure. Against the
eleven-member library the only sub-0.01 margin is the second A-234 acquisition, at 0.0004,
which is also the one surviving class error.

\textbf{What remains is the band set itself, and we do not assign it a direction.}
Replacing positions, intensities and linewidth together leaves the window cosine at 0.794,
not unity, so the largest single component of the deficit is that the two band sets are not
the same set (Figure~\ref{fig:metrics}d). Under one detection criterion applied to both
sides the computed spectra carry on average 13\,\% of the window intensity in bands the
measurement does not show and the measurement 29\,\% in bands the calculation does not,
with GF at 66\,\% and 83\,\%. Neither direction dominates and both rest on two to six
bands per agent, so one unpaired band moves a share by tens of percent; which side has the
surplus is not something eleven molecules can settle. Band position is the largest single
attribute --- replacing it alone lifts the window cosine from 0.630 to 0.736, against 0.677
for intensities and 0.628 for widths --- and for A-230 it carries the agreement from 0.658
to 0.938, so there the error really is one of frequency. GF resists even full replacement
(0.313 to 0.378). A-232 does not: position alone takes it from 0.504 to 0.907, but its
structure was taken from the published IUPAC name and not verified for the vial, and it is
the one material prepared for this work rather than drawn from stock and not assayed. An
impurity is as consistent with its behavior as a structural error, so we do not use A-232
as evidence about the level of theory, and apply to it the reservation we apply to the vial
labeled HN2.

\subsection{The same deficit limits what the method can say about its own reliability}
\label{sec:abstention}

A workflow that misassigns is tolerable if it declines to answer when it should. We tested
whether it can, using three conditions on the same queries: the full computed library, the
library with the query's entire toxicological class removed, and the HN2 acquisitions,
which are out-of-library on the evidence above and not by construction. Six scores were
evaluated --- the top-1 cosine, the margin, a peak-to-sidelobe ratio, a library-normalized
$z$ score, a non-negative unmixing residual and the fraction of measured bands matched ---
with thresholds set by nested leave-one-agent-out so that none is chosen on the queries it
is applied to.

\textbf{No threshold separates the two situations on the computed library without error}
(Figure~\ref{fig:abstention}a--c). The best single score reaches an area under the curve of
0.904 and all six lie between 0.67 and 0.90: the discrimination is not absent, but no
threshold removes the errors. The paired design has to be read with the deletion in mind.
Deleting a class removes candidates, so the class-absent pool is a strict subset of the
full pool; the top-1 cosine is a maximum over that pool, the $z$ score is an affine function
of it, and the unmixing residual is a least-squares residual on a subset of the same basis,
so all three can only move one way and the 26 of 26 they return is a property of the design
and not evidence. The scores free to move either way give a weaker, usable result: the
peak-to-sidelobe ratio orders 24 of 27 acquisitions ($p = 5\times10^{-5}$) and the margin
19 of 26 ($p$ = 0.029), while the band-match fraction fails (13 of 24, $p$ = 0.84). We rest
the paired statement on those two and keep the area under the curve as an effect size
because it is what the open-set literature quotes. The agent-level 95\,\% interval on the
best score is 0.756 to 1.000, whose upper endpoint is a boundary artifact of the bootstrap;
it is a statement about what eight vials establish, not a proof that no score could
separate them. The pooled figure also averages deletions that are not equally hard.
Deleting the Novichok agents is detected by none of the six scores, none reaching 0.81,
whereas deleting the nerve agents or the vesicants is detected by three and five of six; the
first two strata hold only two vials each, so a perfect separation there is what two
clusters against two often produce under an uninformative score, and we report the pattern
as one the design cannot test. Candidate count does not explain it, since the vesicant and
Novichok deletions each leave about fifty candidates and only the second is undetected;
the failure is specific to the A-series, which supplies 14 of the 27 queries and where the
library is also least accurate (Section~S6.4). Driving the class-absent acceptance rate to
zero is possible with five of the six scores, and the cheapest costs 37\,\% of the
legitimate queries: a rule that never accepts a query whose class is missing also refuses
more than a third of those whose class is present, which is not a usable operating point.

\textbf{The same procedure on a measured library succeeds with a single plain threshold}
(Figure~\ref{fig:abstention}d--f). Three of the six scores separate the two conditions
completely, and the result survives restricting the computed library to the same eleven
candidates the measured library offers, so it is not an artifact of candidate count. In the
plain top-1 cosine the populations are disjoint: the lowest value among the 27 queries whose
class is present is 0.842 and the highest among the 27 whose class has been removed is
0.729. We quote the interval and not a single operating value, because a threshold placed
at the maximum of the scores it is then scored against would make the result true by
construction.

\textbf{Abstention shares its cause with identification.} The computed library's similarity
to the correct entry, 0.703, sits inside the distribution of its similarities to incorrect
entries, so no threshold on those similarities separates a library that contains the answer
from one that does not; reliable abstention depends on the accuracy of the calculation and
not on the design of the detector. One limit qualifies this as it qualifies the
identification result: the 0.842 is the similarity of an acquisition to a replicate of the
same vial recorded in the same session, a within-vial repeatability ceiling and not a
library-search figure, and 0.729 is correspondingly an upper bound from one instrument, one
session and one vial per agent. We did not apply the cross-provenance control of
Section~S10.2 to this analysis, so the measured-library result is a statement about the
contrast between the two libraries and not about either threshold as a number to deploy.

\subsection{No repair at the electronic-structure level: theory, geometry or conformer
ensemble}
\label{sec:geometry}
\label{sec:ensemble}

The diagnosis above points at a specific quantity --- the computed position and intensity
of the P--F and P--N stretching motions --- and at three classes of repair: a better
Hamiltonian, a better structure, and a better representation of the liquid. We tested all
three. None recovers the identification, and the last two each uncovered an error that had
been sitting in the library.

\textbf{Three escalations of the level of theory give no generalizable fix.} Implicit
solvation (CPCM), a polarized triple-zeta basis on phosphorus, fluorine, sulfur and
chlorine, and a range-separated functional ($\omega$B97X-D3) were applied in ten
agent--correction pairs across six agents, each swapped into the library with the frequency
scale refitted independently so that no improvement is understated by an inherited
calibration. Three trials improved the 600--850\,\wn{} cosine by more than 0.10, three
degraded it, and one assignment changed (Figure~\ref{fig:theory}a, Section~S7). The larger
phosphorus basis raises GF from 0.583 to 0.772 and turns a confident misassignment to GD
into the correct answer, and the range-separated functional reproduces that correction
independently and more strongly, at 0.924; the same basis change degrades the same window
by 0.081 for GD, cyclosarin's nearest structural neighbor, without carrying it across the
decision boundary. A-232, the worst window in the set at 0.211, remains misassigned under
all three corrections, and its similarity to the correct entry over the full range falls
from 0.636 to 0.624 under the functional that improves its window most. All ten trials
were computed on the pre-correction GF and A-232 geometries, which the next paragraph shows
to be wrong, so the one assignment they repair does not exist in the library this paper
reports; we state the result as a property of the escalation ladder and not of the adopted
library.

\textbf{The shift does not track the error, and no global factor reaches it.} The
basis-set change moves the strongest band of the window upward in three of the six
molecules, by 12.4 to 31.4\,\wn{}, leaves it where it was in one and moves it down in two,
while the baseline errors scatter from $-$43.9 to $+$50.1\,\wn{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:theory}d). A correction of fixed sign can only help the subset whose error
is negative and comparable in size, and among the six only GF qualifies: GD, already within
6.2\,\wn{}, is carried the same distance past the measured band, and A-234, already
50.1\,\wn{} high, is pushed further out. Neither can a global scale factor reach these
errors --- across all ten calculations the refitted factors stay within 1.006--1.024 and
0.980--0.982 and the global band-position RMSE between 11.7 and 12.5\,\wn{}, against
12.0\,\wn{} for the pre-correction library these trials were run against.

\textbf{Two of the eleven computed structures were not at the global minimum, and
correcting them lowers the identification.} A GFN2 metadynamic conformer search from the
published B3LYP geometries leaves nine agents within 1.74\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ of the search
minimum. GF and A-232 are not: reoptimizing both from the search minimum at the same
B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) level and convergence criteria confirms the error at the level of
theory used throughout, by 4.57 and 9.47\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ (3.96 and 9.84 in Gibbs energy at 298\,K), and the rotational constants
change by 7 to 10\,\%, so these are different structures and not reconverged copies of the
same one. Both are true minima with no imaginary frequency. The A-232 error is
configurational rather than conformational: it is an amidine, and the published structure
has the $Z$ arrangement about the C=N bond while its congeners A-230 and A-234 have $E$.
Fixing the conformer within $Z$ lowers the energy by 4.15\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ and
\emph{lowers} the window cosine by 0.053; fixing the configuration lowers the energy a
further 5.32\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ and raises the cosine by 0.300, so the whole gain belongs to
the configuration. Semi-empirical screening ranked the two contributions in the opposite
order, underestimating the amidine $E/Z$ preference by about 3.5\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$: GFN2
energies are adequate to find candidate structures here but not to order the resulting
errors. And a better geometry is not monotonically a better spectrum. For GF the correction
moves the window cosine the wrong way outright, from 0.548 to 0.332, while raising the
adjacent 850--1150\,\wn{} window from 0.730 to 0.919; averaged over the five windows GF
falls from 0.731 to 0.685 and A-232 rises from 0.668 to 0.708. Part of the agreement the
uncorrected library showed in this window was obtained from a structure the material does
not populate.

With both structures replaced and the missing VX calculation supplied, the computed library
assigns 13 of the 30 acquisitions correctly in the closed set and 13 in the open set, the
measured library unchanged at 30 of 30. The pre-correction comparator depends on which
calibration it is scored under and the two defensible choices do not agree, so we give both
rather than choose (Section~S8): the calibration in force before the correction,
$s$ = (1.018, 0.988), gives 17 closed and 16 open and the correction lowers both, while
refitting the same non-circular objective on the pre-correction library, $s$ = (1.022,
0.980), gives 15 and 12, so the correction lowers the closed set and raises the open set by
one. What survives either treatment is that a verified structural error was corrected and
the identification did not recover, and that the closed-set figure fell; we do not claim
the stronger version. Removing VX from the pre-correction library changes no assignment
under either calibration, and none of the three benzene-vial acquisitions is assigned
correctly before or after, so setting them aside reduces each denominator to 27 without
changing a count and leaves the measured library at 27 of 27. The two quality measures do
not move together --- by band position the corrected library is essentially unchanged, the
calibration residual moving from 12.0\,\wn{} over 95 matched pairs to 12.2\,\wn{} over 94,
while by identification it is worse --- and the failures are redistributed rather than
removed: A-234 falls from 1 of 5 to 0 of 5 because the corrected GF and A-232 spectra are
better distractors for it, and all three HN1 acquisitions flip under the refitted scale
factors, holding at 12 of 30 rather than 13 if $s_\mathrm{low}$ is left at the 1.022 the
pre-correction library itself fits instead of the adopted 1.014. A change of 0.8\,\% in one
scale factor therefore moves one query and flips an agent: the identification outcome of
this library is not determined to the precision at which its own calibration is determined,
which is itself a result about the computed library rather than about these two molecules.

\textbf{Boltzmann averaging over the conformer ensemble changes nothing, and exposes a
third structural error.} The library holds one structure per molecule, and for five of the
ten agents no single conformer carries as much as a tenth of the 298\,K population, so
averaging is the rung of the standard ladder with the most room to move. For GF, GD and
A-230 we took the deposited GFN2 ensembles in order of decreasing weight until the
cumulative population reached 80\,\% --- 2, 4 and 9 structures --- and gave each a full
optimization and numerical frequency calculation at the library's own level of theory,
fifteen calculations and sixty-one hours, every one converged with no imaginary frequency.
The weights were then taken from the resulting B3LYP Gibbs energies and not from the GFN2
energies that selected the conformers: the semi-empirical census selects, it does not
weight. The averaged entries give the same 13 of 30 at the molecular level and 26 of 30 at
the class level as the single-conformer ones, and not one of the thirty queries moves.
Averaging itself does almost nothing --- each ensemble lies within 0.005 of its own most
populated conformer in the window. What the calculation finds instead is that all three
library entries are non-minimum on the B3LYP surface, by 0.247 in the window cosine for GF
and by 0.012 and 0.003 for A-230 and GD, while averaging over the remaining populated
conformers contributes at most 0.005; the small window gains for GD and A-230 are therefore
structure changes partly offset by averaging in the opposite direction, and not averaging
effects. GF is the same decomposition with a large first term, which makes it a reportable
error rather than a rounding: its window cosine rises from 0.323 to 0.569 and its
full-range similarity from 0.517 to 0.678, essentially all of it belonging to the one
conformer the GFN2 census ranked second. That census gives the structure the library holds
0.719 of the two-conformer weight; at the library's own level of theory it carries 0.032
against 0.968, the other structure lying 1.68\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ lower in energy and
2.02\,kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ lower in Gibbs energy. The two surfaces disagree on which structure
GF is, so the GF entry adopted above after the first correction is still not the minimum at
the level of theory used throughout --- the third structural error in this work and the
second in GF --- and the agreement available from the right structure alone is 0.570
against the 0.323 the library delivers. The ensemble calculation found it where the census
could not, which is the conclusion the A-232 amidine reached by a different route.
Correcting it changes no assignment either: all three GF acquisitions remain assigned to
soman.

\textbf{What this establishes.} The failures of the computed library are not bookkeeping.
Three of them were genuine structural errors, found and corrected at the level of theory
used throughout, and the corrections move the agreement and not the assignment; they
redistribute which agents fail. Taken with the metric and lineshape controls reported
above, the deficit survives every repair to the electronic-structure level available to us.
We report the ensemble test as one rung on three agents and not as a general result: eight
agents are untested, among them the four for which the census suggests averaging matters
most, and anharmonic treatment and frequency-dependent polarizability derivatives remain
untested throughout. The per-trial and per-structure energies, the window-resolved
comparisons, the implicit-solvation negative control, the sensitivity to the band-pairing
rule and the full calibration-sensitivity analysis are given in the Supplementary Material
(S7, S8).

\subsection{With the query's own molecule absent, class assignment falls to 19 of 27}
\label{sec:lomo}

Every figure reported so far is closed-set: each query's own computed entry is in the
library it is searched against. That is not the condition a computed library is built for.
The reason to compute a reference is that none has been measured, so the molecule in front
of the instrument is the one the library does not hold. The Analysis section declares
the test of that condition --- each agent's own computed entry deleted before its
acquisitions are queried, and the class of the best remaining match recorded --- and we
report it here. Only the class level has an answer, since with the entry deleted there is
no correct molecular entry to find.

\textbf{Class assignment falls from 26 of 27 to 19 of 27} (Section~S6.1 of the
Supplementary Material). On the agent as the unit the figure depends on which agent-level
rule is used, and we give both: requiring every acquisition of an agent to be class-correct
it falls from 7 of 8 to 4 of 8, and on the majority rule used elsewhere in this work from 8
of 8 to 6 of 8. A paired exact test on the stricter unit gives $b$ = 3, $c$ = 0, two-sided
$p$ = 0.250, and on the majority unit $b$ = 2, $c$ = 0, $p$ = 0.500: the direction is
consistent and the design has no power to establish it, exactly as at the molecular level.
The measured library under the same deletion is class-correct for 27 of 27.

\textbf{Part of the drop is candidate count, and part is not.} Deleting one entry from the
54-member computed library leaves 53 candidates, 43 of them organophosphorus distractors
with no counterpart in the eleven-member measured library, so the two arms are not
comparable as they stand. Restricting the computed library to the same eleven entries the
measured library offers, so that both go from eleven candidates to ten, gives 22 of 27
rather than 19, and 6 of 8 agents on the strict rule or 7 of 8 on the majority rule. Three
of the four non-soman errors are therefore captures by distractors. The control does not
improve the picture that matters, however, and in one respect it makes it worse. Of the
eight errors against the full library, four are crossings into the vesicant class; of the
five that survive the control, all five are. Equalizing the candidate count removes three
errors, and every one it removes is a crossing within the organophosphorus agents, so what
is left is soman assigned to a vesicant in all four of its acquisitions and A-234 crossing
to the computed HN1 entry. The error rate falls and the rate of errors that would invert a
prophylaxis decision rises, from four of twenty-seven to five.

\textbf{The surviving loss is in the direction that matters.} All four acquisitions of
soman are assigned to the computed nitrogen-mustard entry, a nerve-agent-to-vesicant
crossing, and that is the error type that inverts a prophylaxis decision. The other four
errors are Novichok acquisitions assigned to organophosphorus analogs, which is a crossing
within the organophosphorus anticholinesterases, so the immediate treatment it implies is
the same and we do not count it as a decision error. Two of the eight agents, soman and
A-230, account for six of the eight errors.

\textbf{No confidence score flags them.} Over the deleted-molecule condition the top-1
cosine of the class-correct acquisitions runs 0.650 to 0.871 with a median of 0.777 and
that of the class-incorrect ones 0.648 to 0.793 with a median of 0.787. The wrong answers
are, if anything, the more confident, and seven of the eight sit above the lowest
class-correct value, so no threshold on that score separates them. This is the same
finding as Section~\ref{sec:abstention} in a different and more realistic condition: the
score that would have to carry the abstention decision does not have the accuracy to carry
it.

\subsection{What these numbers support in practice}

Three statements survive for a laboratory that has to decide what to do with a portable
1064\,nm instrument and no measured reference for the agent in front of it.

\textbf{Class is usable only where the molecule is already in the library.} With every
entry present the computed library is class-correct for 26 of 30 acquisitions (26 of 27
without the benzene vial; 8 of 8 agents), indistinguishable from the measured library on
these data. That describes a laboratory whose library already holds the agent, which is not
the situation a computed library is built for. With the query's own entry deleted, class
assignment falls to 19 of 27 and 4 of 8 agents (Section~\ref{sec:lomo}): soman is read as
a nitrogen mustard in all four acquisitions, and the top-1 cosine does not separate wrong
answers from right ones. A computed library can therefore support a class decision about a
molecule it already contains, which is a completeness claim and not an identification
claim, and cannot be relied on for one it does not. The agent-level figure rests on eight
species (exact binomial interval 0.63 to 1.00); the one surviving closed-set class error, an
A-234 acquisition assigned to HN1, is a Novichok-to-vesicant crossing that would change a
prophylaxis decision and that we cannot attribute to the sample
(Section~\ref{sec:headtohead}); and at the molecular level the 13 of 30 is what an
identification claim is worth.

\textbf{A computed library is a screening prior, not an answer.} The correct molecule is in
the top three of the 54 candidates for 22 of 30 queries (22 of 27 without the benzene vial),
a usable reduction of the candidate set to hand to a confirmatory method. The figure is
closed-set and this study reports no false-positive rate, so the prior is conditional on
the material being in the library and cannot be read as a screening result for an unknown;
and a hit list from a computed library has no standing under the Convention's identification
criteria and must not be recorded as supporting evidence in a verification context.

\textbf{A measured library's confidence does not certify its own entries.} The three largest
decision margins in this work belong to the vial whose contents contradict its label,
because it is spectrally isolated --- its similarity to every other measured entry is at
most 0.168, where HN1 and HN3 sit at 0.935 against each other --- and not because it is
right. A high top-1 confidence against a measured library means the query matches the
reference and nothing more. The pre-registered quality criterion flagged this vial on
measured evidence alone, and the computed library explained it, through a self-similarity of
0.084 and the benzene band assignment: a second use for a computed library, an independent
check on the provenance of a measured reference set. It is not a general capability on this
evidence, since the abstention score with the best class-absent separation accepts this
vial in all three acquisitions while the plain top-1 cosine rejects it.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}

\textbf{The measurement set is small and singly sourced.} Eleven agents, one instrument,
one campaign, one vial per agent, all acquisitions within a single hour. Nine agents
carry three to five genuine acquisitions and two carry one. Day-to-day reproducibility
is untested by this design, and the measured-library results should be read as an upper
bound obtained under maximally favorable conditions and not as a transferable
performance figure.

\textbf{What the measured library delivers on queries of another provenance is roughly
half of its figure here, and the contrast with the computed library survives at that
level.} The only external queries available for authentic agents at this excitation are
the band lists Kondo et al.\ publish for the seven agents in common. We used them as
queries against our two libraries. A self-sparsified control, which reduces each agent's
own measured spectrum to its published band count, separates the effect of sparsity from
that of provenance. The measured library is correct for three to five of the seven and the
computed library for two to three. On the five agents with more than one acquisition, for
which the control is not circular, the comb cosine gives three of five against two of five
(Section~S10.2 of the Supplementary Material). Two things follow. The 30 of 30 of
Section~\ref{sec:headtohead} is a within-vial repeatability ceiling and not what a
measured library delivers on material it has not itself measured; and the direction of the
comparison survives the change of provenance, at a ratio nearer three to two than
thirty to thirteen. We regard the second as the more transferable statement of the
result and the first as the controlled one.

\textbf{No false-positive rate is claimed.} Every query in this study is a chemical
warfare agent. The behavior of either library on the non-agent materials that dominate
real use, organophosphorus pesticides above all, is untested, and a method that has
not been shown a negative is not yet an identification method.

\textbf{The study is qualitative.} No calibration curves, limits of detection or dynamic
ranges are reported.\cite{witkiewicz2018analysis} All measurements are of neat material.

\textbf{One sample is not the neat material the protocol specifies.} The vial labeled HN2 gives a computed self-similarity of 0.084, and its four strong bands are those of liquid benzene; two of the four bands published for HN2 at this excitation are present beneath them, at three standard deviations or better in every acquisition.
We report the identification results with and without it. We have not established what
the vial contains.

\section{Conclusions}

Thirty acquisitions of nine sealed vials of authentic agent were queried against a measured
and a calibrated 54-member computed Raman library under one pipeline, one native grid and
one metric, with no calibration parameter fitted to the identification. The measured
library assigns all thirty at the molecular level and the computed library thirteen, 12 to
19 across the forward model's declared free choices. The measured arm is a within-vial
repeatability ceiling rather than a library-search figure: on another laboratory's
published band lists the two reach three of five and two of five, and that ratio, not
30 against 13, is what transfers.

What fails is accuracy on the correct molecule, not separation between candidates. The
similarity to the correct entry falls from 0.983 to 0.703 while congener similarities are
comparable, the deficit concentrates in the 600--850\,\wn{} window built from P--F, P=O,
P--O and P--C stretching, at a band-position residual of 0.55 of the adopted linewidth overall --- the regime in which similarity measures are reported to invert --- and with computed and measured band sets that differ. Those are the quantities a computed library has to improve, and they are the transferable requirement this work establishes: not that harmonic density functional theory is inadequate in general, but that a forward model must bring its band positions well inside its own linewidth and reproduce the measured band set before library search can be expected to identify a molecule.

No intervention tested here reaches it. Twelve metrics recover at most three of the
seventeen errors; three escalations of the level of theory move individual agents and not
the aggregate; two verified structural errors, corrected at the level of theory used
throughout, move the agreement and not the assignment; Boltzmann averaging over the
conformer ensembles of three agents changes no assignment and reveals a third non-minimum
structure; and scaling resolved by mode type, the form the literature prefers, returns the
same thirteen of thirty on a worse fit. Anharmonicity is untested, and the sign-varying
per-molecule residuals are not what a one-signed correction produces.

Three boundaries travel with the result and should not be separated from it. The class
level is not a general escape: with every entry present the computed library is
class-correct for 26 of 27, but delete the query's own entry --- the case a computed
reference exists to cover --- and it falls to 19 of 27, or 22 of 27 once the candidate
count is equalized, with soman read as a vesicant in all four of its acquisitions and no
confidence score separating the wrong answers from the right ones. Abstention fails for
the same cause rather than for a separate one: of six scores none separates the
class-absent condition without error, and forcing that rate to zero costs at best 37\,\%
of the legitimate queries. And the study is qualitative throughout, on eleven materials
none of which was assayed for identity or purity, one of which was found to be benzene; so
a top-1 library match here is not a confirmatory identification in the sense the
Convention's verification practice requires, and every headline figure is reported both
with and without the vial whose contents are not established.

\section*{Responsible disclosure}

This manuscript reports detection and identification data. It contains no synthetic
route, no precursor, no purification procedure, no quantity or scale-up information, and
no formulation or dissemination information for any scheduled chemical. The molecular
structures used in the calculations are those already in the open literature, and the
organophosphorus analog grid is a set of computational distractors constructed from
substituent patterns that are standard textbook chemistry. Some members of that grid are
themselves Schedule 1 chemicals, and the deposited library gives a calibrated predicted
line list for each. We judge the uplift to be nil, for the reason this paper reports: those
predicted spectra do not reach molecular identification even for the agents against which
they were calibrated. The deposit carries that statement at the head of the library so that
no reader mistakes it for a usable search library. The principal result is
negative: a library computed from molecular structures does not substitute for measured
reference spectra.

The measured arm requires its own assessment, because the argument above is about the
computed library and the measured library is the one that works. The deposit contains the
preprocessed measured spectra of all eleven vials, including the four A-series agents, for
which no measured reference exists elsewhere in the open literature; that release was made
by an OPCW designated laboratory under its own procedures. A Raman reference spectrum
supports the recognition of material already in hand. It carries no route, precursor,
quantity, purity or formulation information, and nothing in it assists the synthesis or
acquisition of an agent by a party that does not already possess one. The capability it
confers is the defensive one this work exists to serve: a laboratory without access to
authentic material can now recognize these agents, which is the gap the manuscript opens
by describing. We judge that balance to favor release and we state the judgment rather
than leave it implied. Deposition in the OPCW Central Analytical Database would be the
controlled channel for reference spectra of this kind, and these data are not eligible for
it, because that database admits spectra only for material the depositing laboratory has
itself confirmed by two independent techniques and no orthogonal confirmation was
performed here. The open deposit is not a substitute for that channel and we do not
present it as one; it exists so that the analysis in this paper can be checked, and the
raw acquisition records remain with the facility.

The authors judge that the work provides no uplift to the synthesis,
acquisition or use of chemical weapons, and that its publication serves the defensive
purpose of improving detection and verification. Raw acquisition files of the authentic
agents are retained under the facility's own records rather than placed in an open archive,
as stated under Data availability.
